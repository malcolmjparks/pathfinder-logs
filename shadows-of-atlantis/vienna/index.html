Vienna

Act One: A Strange Request

The agents meet Gisela Waltrun in a cafe, where she makes small talk. Louis tries to hide but she notices him. She also appears to have a split lip. She leaves, but glances down meaningfully at her tip. Garance finds there a note with instructions to meet that evening at the Prater.

At the Prater, they find Gisela sitting at a table listening to some live music. She dances with Ralph, and takes to opportunity to tell him why she really contacted Section D - her fiancé Dr. Botho Ehrlichmann - died in an "accident", but she knows he distrusted his superiors and now she distrusts her too. She is part of the German intelligence, because she thinks both they and the German government have been infiltrated by groups with ulterior motives. She drops her purse and takes the opportunity to pass a couple of pieces of paper to Ralph. She makes her excuses and leaves.

Looking at the papers, they find a letter to Gisela warning of possible trouble and the first page of a journal to do with Ehrlichmann's research translating the Black Stone of Sais. Detecting the smell of lemon on the letter, they find a hidden note referring to the Mexican apothecary's bookshop, and a symbol they don't recognise.

Act Two: Chaos & Truth

The next day, they ask around various bookshops and are eventually directed to the bookshop owned by Ernst Pretzsche - an odious man who is a Nazi sympathiser but noticeably upset with them for killing his promising young acolyte and friend. He is unconvinced by the agents, but agrees to help them in return for a favour to be repaid when he chooses. He passes them a note with the famous Boltzmann formula on it and tells them his tomb is in Vienna's largest cemetery. He also recognises the symbol in the hidden note as belonging to the "Sphinx Reading Club", which he is very dismissive of.

Going to the cemetery in the day, the agents notice a patch of disturbed earth nearby, and plan to come back at night. At night, they dig up the disturbed earth and discover a journal with the first page missing and a small statuette. At that moment, they are attacked by four heavies, and Calvin notices someone smoking a cigarette and watching on. The other agents take down the heavies, and Calvin injures the smoking man before he runs away into the darkness.

The agents rest and have a brief look at Ehrlichmann's journal - they can tell it will take some time to interpret. In the meantime they get Ehrlichmann's Sphinx membership card from Gisela, who also identifies the man that attacked them as Gotthold Fuchs, and make a trip to the reading club. They take a look around and don't find anything at Ehrlichmann's desk, but in Fuchs' desk they find memos detailing the movements of some agents around Vienna and the orders to take care of "our Viennese archaeologist". Now sure it was Fuchs who was responsible, they report to Gisela who thanks them for injuring Fuchs and driving him away.

Act Three: Enlightenment

Gisela advises them to leave Vienna, as tensions are escalating. A first read of the journal gives the following hints:

  • Ehrlichmann began translating the Black Stone, trying to trace the ancient origins of the Germanic peoples.
  • There appears to be some sort of powerful ancient artefact that was split up in antiquity and the parts hidden in five far-flung locations scattered across the globe.
  • One of these locations is the Eternal City itself: Rome.
  • The artefacts are reportedly from the lost civilisation of Atlantis.
  • A German expedition has recently brought back a manuscript from Tibet that corroborates the information found on the Black Stone.
  • Ehrlichmann is convinced that someone within the Wehrmacht is determined to collect the artefacts and intends to use them as the basis of weapons research for the Third Reich.

Meanwhile, they also get a coded telegram from Lieutenant Strang - "Pasquino has lost his voice . His dearest Lucrezia is concerned for his health. There are too many sausages in the pantry. Please come home and help your sister . She cannot run the family restaurant alone." Strang thinks their two Section D agents in Rome (codenames Pasquino and Lucrezia) are in trouble, and the Germans have something to do with it.

With these facts in mind, the agents decide to catch a train to Rome - and study the journal further as they travel.
